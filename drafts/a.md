OPEN INTEREST - DAILY EDITION - 16 November 2026

LEAD

Attackers redirected Luxor customers' bitcoin hashrate to a pool they controlled.

Luxor says it patched the flaw within two hours and will reimburse just over a dozen affected customers. Reporting describes the attack as AI-enabled, a claim that rests on the company's own account.

Bitcoin mining machines do not hold coins. They sell work to a pool, and the pool pays whoever the configuration names. Over the weekend of 14-15 November 2026, attackers changed that configuration for some Luxor customers [https://cointelegraph.com/news/luxor-ceo-warns-of-ai-dark-forest-after-bitcoin-miner-hijack].

According to Cointelegraph, the attackers worked through Luxor's Commander platform, the software customers use to manage their machines [https://cointelegraph.com/news/luxor-ceo-warns-of-ai-dark-forest-after-bitcoin-miner-hijack]. CoinDesk reports that the affected machines were pointed at another mining pool, so their bitcoin rewards went to the attacker [https://www.coindesk.com/tech/2026/11/16/bitcoin-miners-were-hijacked-and-their-btc-rewards-redirected-to-an-attacker].

The scale, on Luxor's telling, is small. CoinDesk reports Luxor's statement that just over a dozen customers were affected and will be reimbursed [https://www.coindesk.com/tech/2026/11/16/bitcoin-miners-were-hijacked-and-their-btc-rewards-redirected-to-an-attacker]. The Block reports Luxor's statements that the vulnerability was patched within two hours and that no data was leaked or exfiltrated [https://www.theblock.co/news/business/2026-11-15-luxor-attack-client-hashrate-440810].

The method is the larger claim. The Block characterizes the incident as an AI-enabled attack, and Cointelegraph reports that Luxor's chief executive warned crypto businesses of an AI "dark forest" [https://www.theblock.co/news/business/2026-11-15-luxor-attack-client-hashrate-440810] [https://cointelegraph.com/news/luxor-ceo-warns-of-ai-dark-forest-after-bitcoin-miner-hijack]. No independent forensic report has been published, so the AI attribution remains Luxor's assessment.

What is missing matters. The published summaries give no figure for hashrate diverted, bitcoin lost or the receiving pool [https://www.coindesk.com/tech/2026/11/16/bitcoin-miners-were-hijacked-and-their-btc-rewards-redirected-to-an-attacker]. Until Luxor publishes them, customers have a promise of reimbursement and no measure of the loss.

TECH

[TECH] Vitalik Buterin argues mempool aggregation can absorb quantum-safe signatures.
In a post on 16 November 2026, Buterin wrote that quantum-safe signatures grow from 65 bytes to 1-6 kilobytes, and STARK proofs reach about 128 kilobytes [https://api.fxtwitter.com/VitalikButerin/status/2122233780371083553]. He called that "unsustainable to put on chain" and said EIP-8288 would have nodes bundle such objects into aggregate STARKs in the mempool [https://api.fxtwitter.com/VitalikButerin/status/2122233780371083553]. Decrypt reports that Buterin co-authored the draft with Thomas Coratger in June 2026 [https://decrypt.co/383294/vitalik-buterin-now-a-total-bull-on-leanpcs-as-fast-track-to-quantum-safe-ethereum]. The proposal is a draft, not a scheduled upgrade.

[TECH] A Scroll verifier bug let caller bytes choose which function ran.
Zealynx wrote on 16 November 2026 that Scroll registered a new proof verifier on mainnet on 10 November 2026 after a bug bounty report [https://ethereum-magicians.org/t/30405]. The old contract copied the first bytes of a caller-supplied proof into the call, so a proof starting with 0x24270d54 reached a different function [https://ethereum-magicians.org/t/30405]. Citing Scroll's report, the author says an old valid proof could pass for new public input, but exploiting it required the prover role or enforced batch mode [https://ethereum-magicians.org/t/30405]. Scroll checked all 748 finalizations made while the code was live and found none with the prefix, the post says [https://ethereum-magicians.org/t/30405].
